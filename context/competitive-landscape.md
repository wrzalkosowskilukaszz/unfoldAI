# Competitive landscape

Researched Aug 2026. Refresh before making positioning decisions on it.

## Brieflow — brieflow.co

The closest competitor. *"Send Quick Design Briefs to Your Clients — get clear
project details in 2 minutes so you can start designing."*

- Whole product is three steps: send client a **link** → client fills a form on
  any device, **no account** → responses land in the creative's dashboard.
- Next.js + Supabase. `<title>` is still "Create Next App".
- Five pages total. No pricing page, no entity, no trademark notice.
- **No evidence of any AI** — nothing in the marketing, nothing in the privacy
  policy's processor list.

**Why they matter:** they own the client-facing link, which Surveyvor has no
equivalent for. In Surveyvor the *designer* types what the client said.

**Why we beat them:** a form is only as good as whoever fills it. A client who
doesn't know what they want fills a form badly, and Brieflow hands the designer
that confusion neatly structured. Structured garbage is still garbage.

**Do not compete on speed.** Their "2 minutes" is only achievable *because* they
interrogate nothing. Racing them to fast deletes our reason to exist.

## Content Snare — contentsnare.com

Mature analogue (client information collection for agencies). Mechanics worth
knowing:

| Mechanic | Status here |
|---|---|
| Auto-save so clients needn't finish in one sitting | **Taken** — save indicator |
| Reject individual fields with a comment | **Taken** — dismissable findings |
| Templates | **Taken and extended** — templates carry a knowledge model |
| Automated reminders / chasing | **Refused** — needs accounts + email, breaks local-only |
| Progress visible to the other party | Needs the client link. Not built. |

## HolaBrief — holabrief.com

Creative brief tool for designers. Surfaced during naming research. Not analysed
in depth; relevant mainly as evidence the "brief" namespace is crowded.

## Still not built

- **Client-facing link.** Architecturally incompatible with local-only: needs a
  backend, designer accounts, server-stored briefs, and holding *clients' clients'*
  data (GDPR, retention, a legal entity). It is a different product, not a feature.
- **Client-side file upload.** Brieflow accepts uploads from the client.
- **A lower wall at the start.** Eight steps is a lot cold. A quick-start that
  asks three questions and infers the rest would lower it without losing depth.
